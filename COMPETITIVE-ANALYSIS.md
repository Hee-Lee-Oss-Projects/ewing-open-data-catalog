# Competitive & Improvement Analysis — `ewing-open-data-catalog`

> Scope: an open, license-clear **INDEX + datasheets** of *open* Ewing sarcoma datasets (GEO series, cBioPortal Ewing studies, ICGC open tier, Treehouse, and — to be verified — TARGET/GDC). Deliverable is **metadata about data, never the data**. Cancer guardrails: open-access/aggregate/de-identified only; controlled-access (dbGaP/EGA/DACO) and identifiable patient data out of scope; per-source license verification; provenance; re-identification gate.
>
> Analysis date: 2026-06-29. Sources cited inline; the plan reviewed is `PLAN.md` v0.1.0 + `TASKS.md`.

---

## 1. Correctness & completeness review of PLAN.md

The plan is unusually rigorous for a v0.1 draft — the three-part blocking gate (access-tier → license → re-identification), the canonical-model-first design, the "documentation not data" boundary, and the honest `verifiedNeed: false` posture are all strong and correct. The findings below are gaps, errors, or weak spots, ordered roughly by importance.

**1.1 TARGET almost certainly holds no open Ewing cohort — the plan flags this but still over-weights TARGET (CONFIRMED by research).** NCI's TARGET program projects are ALL, AML, neuroblastoma, osteosarcoma, Wilms tumor, rhabdoid tumors, and CCSK — **Ewing sarcoma is not a TARGET project** ([TARGET — About the Program, NCI](https://www.cancer.gov/ccg/research/genome-sequencing/target/about); [TARGET on Registry of Open Data on AWS](https://registry.opendata.aws/target/)). The plan's "honest TARGET flag" (Improvement #5, `coverage-005`) is correct to demand verification, but TARGET is still listed as a first-class source family in the executive summary, goals ("source adapters … GDC/ICGC open tier"), and the M2 `gdc-017` adapter. **Recommendation:** treat TARGET as the *expected-negative* case in `coverage-005` and re-scope `gdc-017` so its open-Ewing value comes from ICGC/GDC's bone-cancer projects (e.g. ICGC **BOCA-FR / "Bone Cancer"**, which includes Ewing) rather than TARGET. Where Ewing genomics *does* live in GDC is the **CGCI** umbrella and ad-hoc sarcoma studies — not TARGET. This is the single most concrete factual correction.

**1.2 The candidate-source list is materially incomplete — several real open-Ewing homes are missing.** The named families (GEO, TARGET, Treehouse, cBioPortal, ICGC) omit sources that genuinely hold open or aggregate Ewing data: **St. Jude Cloud / PeCan** (open curated pediatric variants, >5,000 genomes; [St. Jude Cloud](https://www.stjude.cloud/), [PeCan docs](https://docs.stjude.cloud/pecan/overview/getting-started)); **DepMap / CCLE** (open Ewing *cell-line* dependency + omics data, CC-BY 4.0 on AWS; [DepMap CCLE on AWS Open Data](https://registry.opendata.aws/depmap-omics-ccle/)); **UCSC Xena** (hosts Treehouse + ICGC + cohort-level Ewing as 800+ public datasets; [Xena](https://cgl.genomics.ucsc.edu/xena/)); and the canonical open Ewing genomics papers' GEO/EGA-split deposits (Crompton 2014, Tirode 2014, Brohl 2014). A pediatric rare cancer has *few* open cohorts, so missing even two is significant for a 6-dataset target. **Recommendation:** add St. Jude PeCan and DepMap/CCLE as named candidate families; note that many cell-line datasets sidestep the re-identification gate entirely (no patient, no germline), making them the *cheapest* high-confidence PASSes for the pilot.

**1.3 The plan does not position against its single most direct competitor — NCI's CCDI Childhood Cancer Data Catalog.** The CCDI Catalog is *already* "an inventory of childhood cancer data repositories … filterable by repository, registry, data content type," currently **222 datasets** ([CCDI Childhood Cancer Data Catalog](https://datacatalog.ccdi.cancer.gov/); [NCI announcement](https://www.cancer.gov/about-nci/organization/cbiit/news-events/news/2025/ccdi-central-hub-links-researchers-childhood-cancer-data-resources)). It even has dedicated resource pages for Treehouse, Xena, cBioPortal, and DepMap. A plan whose headline deliverable is "a license-clear index of open Ewing datasets" must explicitly differentiate from a federally funded catalog that overlaps heavily on the *catalog* axis. The differentiator is real (CCDI is *resource-level* pointers, not *per-dataset* license-verified datasheets with cited `permitsDerivatives` clauses and re-id assessments) — but the plan never names CCDI, which is a competitive-awareness gap, not just a documentation one. **Recommendation:** add CCDI to References and Risks; frame the catalog as "datasheet-depth layer on top of resource-level catalogs like CCDI," and consider *contributing entries back to CCDI* as a ready-made adoption channel (directly addresses the `verifiedNeed: false` problem).

**1.4 GEO "public domain (US Gov)" is an oversimplification that could license-misclassify.** The plan says "NCBI content is generally public domain (US Gov), but individual submitter terms … must be checked" — the caveat is right, but the framing risks a reviewer treating GEO as default-open. NCBI/NLM *infrastructure and NLM-authored metadata* are US-Gov public domain; **submitter-deposited data and its descriptions carry no blanket license and are not automatically public domain** (NCBI's own policy notes some data may be subject to third-party rights). For a metadata catalog this mostly matters for *redistributing the submitter's description text*. **Recommendation:** in `policy-004`, make the GEO default "record provenance + attribute submitter; treat the *summary/abstract* text as the submitter's, not PD" rather than "GEO = public domain." This is the most likely place a license error slips in (the plan's own target is <1 license error per 10).

**1.5 The re-identification gate is well-designed but arguably mis-scoped for a metadata-only product, and the k<5 threshold is asserted without a cited basis.** Because the catalog never redistributes individual-level data, the *catalog's own* re-identification risk is near-zero; the gate is really vouching that the **source** is appropriately open and that the catalog won't surface a finer cross-tab than the publisher did. That is a sound conservative posture, but the plan conflates "is this dataset safe to *reuse*" (the publisher's responsibility, governed by their access tier) with "is our *documentation* safe" (ours). The `k<5` small-cell rule is reasonable but cites no standard — HIPAA Safe Harbor, the common cell-suppression conventions (often k<5 or k<11 in US public-health releases), or a consortium policy. **Recommendation:** (a) state explicitly that the re-id check is a *source-appropriateness* check, not a transformation we perform; (b) cite a basis for the threshold and allow it to be policy-tunable per source. Keep the gate — just sharpen what it asserts so reviewers apply it consistently.

**1.6 Croissant ML is an ML-dataset format and is *not* the most natural biomedical-catalog standard — FAIR/DCAT/DATS/Bioschemas alignment is under-specified.** Croissant v1.0 (MLCommons, released March 2024) is a schema.org extension that makes datasets discoverable via **Google Dataset Search** ([MLCommons Croissant announcement](https://mlcommons.org/2024/03/croissant_metadata_announce/)) — genuinely useful for the "Findable" leg of FAIR. But Croissant is oriented to ML training assets (record sets, fields, splits), whereas biomedical *dataset catalogs* conventionally use **schema.org/Dataset + Bioschemas**, **DCAT**, or **DATS** (the Data Tag Suite used by NIH's bioCADDIE/DataMed). OmicsDI, a direct analog, encodes every dataset in **schema.org** specifically so Google Dataset Search can index it ([OmicsDI](https://www.omicsdi.org/), [EMBL-EBI on OmicsDI/FAIR](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/about/news/announcements/omicsdi-2017/)). Emitting *only* Croissant risks being ML-shaped and missing the biomedical discovery ecosystem. **Recommendation:** emit **schema.org/Dataset (+ Bioschemas profile) as the primary record and Croissant as a secondary projection** — the canonical-model-first design already supports multiple projections, so this is cheap and materially widens "Findable."

**1.7 Dataset versioning & DOIs: the index gets a Zenodo DOI, but per-source dataset versioning is hand-waved.** GEO accessions (GSExxxxx) are versioned only loosely and issue no DOIs; cBioPortal studies are versioned by opaque data-freeze; ICGC releases are numbered. The `provenance.version` field exists, but the plan doesn't say how "version" is *determined* per source (retrieval date is a weak proxy). **Recommendation:** define a per-source version key (GEO: accession + last-update date; cBioPortal: study identifier + portal data-version; ICGC: release number) in `coverage-005`/`policy-004`, so `refresh-020` drift detection has something deterministic to diff.

**1.8 Metrics: the headline outcome ("adopted by a named beneficiary") is fragile and the completeness score risks being a vanity metric in disguise.** The plan is admirably honest that "delivered ≠ merged," but *every* success metric ultimately gates on a partner that does not exist (`verifiedNeed: false` throughout). The Zenodo-DOI self-publish fallback is the only thing keeping M0 deliverable, and self-publishing a DOI is *not* third-party adoption — the plan even says "self-published" counts as a delivered outcome, which is in tension with "adopted by a beneficiary." The 0–100 completeness score measures *our thoroughness*, not *reuse* — exactly the "datasheets written" vanity metric the plan claims to exclude, relabeled. **Recommendation:** make CCDI-contribution or a sibling-project import the *primary* adoption path (both are concretely reachable), and demote completeness to a quality gate, not a success metric.

**1.9 Minor / mechanical.**
- "Croissant v1.0 … validate against the v1.0 JSON-LD context/SHACL" — confirm Croissant ships a SHACL shapes file you can pin; the official tooling is a Python validator (`mlcommons-croissant`) + JSON-LD context. Don't assert SHACL if the validator path is procedural.
- COSMIC/OncoKB correctly excluded as non-commercial — accurate; both are NC. Keep.
- The plan lists `deliverable: dataset` as a schema enum but commits never to use it; `TASKS.md` line 22 still documents `dataset` as allowed while pilot/doc tasks use `type: data` with `deliverable: document`. Slight friction between `type` and `deliverable` naming — harmless but worth a one-line note that `type: data` never implies shipping data.
- TARGET appears in the `gdc-017` adapter ACs (M2) even though `coverage-005` (M0) may drop it — sequence risk: don't build a TARGET path before coverage verification resolves it.

## 2. Competitive landscape

Real, cited competitors and adjacent resources. None do exactly what this project proposes (per-dataset, license-verified, re-id-assessed *datasheets* for *rare-cancer* open data) — but several overlap on discovery, hosting, or catalog axes.

**cBioPortal** — open-source portal aggregating >350 cancer genomics studies (TCGA, CCLE, consortia) with OncoPrints, mutation plots, survival analysis; >35,000 monthly users ([cBioPortal](https://www.cbioportal.org/); [cBio paper, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3956037/)). *Strengths:* canonical home of several open Ewing studies; per-study download; strong analysis UI. *Weaknesses:* per-study licensing is heterogeneous and **not surfaced in a machine-readable, license-clear way**; no datasheets; no re-id/consent provenance; finding "which studies are truly open + derivable" still requires manual study-by-study work — exactly the gap this project fills.

**NCI Genomic Data Commons (GDC)** — NCI's harmonized repository for TCGA, TARGET, CGCI, etc., with explicit **open vs controlled** tiers (open = high-level non-identifiable; controlled = germline/low-level, dbGaP-gated) ([GDC Data Access Policies](https://gdc.cancer.gov/access-data/data-access-policies)). *Strengths:* authoritative access-tier designations (the catalog's primary evidence source); harmonized. *Weaknesses:* Ewing coverage is thin/absent in TARGET (see 1.1); the open/controlled split is per-file and easy to mis-read — precisely the controlled-as-open hazard the plan's gate guards against.

**UCSC Xena** — federated hubs + browser hosting **800+ public datasets** (TCGA, ICGC, Treehouse, CCLE, PCAWG; 1,500+ across hubs) with visualization and survival analysis ([UCSC Xena](https://cgl.genomics.ucsc.edu/xena/); [Xena platform, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/326470v6)). *Strengths:* aggregates Treehouse + ICGC pediatric/cohort data in one place; great for *exploration*. *Weaknesses:* a visualization layer, not a license/provenance catalog; no datasheets or derivability verification.

**Treehouse Childhood Cancer Initiative (UCSC)** — public pediatric expression compendium, **>12,000 samples** with clinical fields, derived partly from TARGET/TCGA ([Treehouse Public Data](https://treehousegenomics.ucsc.edu/public-data/); [CCDI resource page](https://datacatalog.ccdi.cancer.gov/resource/Treehouse)). *Strengths:* directly relevant pediatric open expression data. *Weaknesses:* commonly carries a **data-use agreement/registration** even when "public" — exactly the `dataUseConditions` nuance the plan captures; terms are not machine-readable.

**St. Jude Cloud / PeCan** — pediatric genomic ecosystem: controlled-access raw Genomics Platform **plus open PeCan** knowledgebase (curated somatic variants from >5,000 genomes) and Visualization Community ([St. Jude Cloud](https://www.stjude.cloud/); [St. Jude Cloud paper, Cancer Discovery](https://aacrjournals.org/cancerdiscovery/article/11/5/1082/666431/)). *Strengths:* a clean open/controlled split (PeCan open, raw controlled) — a textbook case for the plan's "document the open part, exclude the controlled raw tier" open question (1.4 of the plan). *Weaknesses:* not in the plan's source list (gap 1.2); PeCan terms are portal-specific.

**ICGC / ICGC-ARGO** — international consortium with **open and DACO-controlled** tiers; pan-cancer including bone-cancer/Ewing projects ([phs/TARGET access note via GDC]; ICGC-ARGO portal). *Strengths:* a genuine open-tier home for some Ewing genomics (BOCA). *Weaknesses:* open/controlled boundary requires DACO literacy; attribution required — correctly noted by the plan.

**DepMap / CCLE (Broad)** — open cancer **cell-line** dependency maps (CRISPR/RNAi) + omics; includes Ewing lines; CC-BY 4.0 release on AWS ([DepMap](https://depmap.org/portal/); [CCLE on AWS Open Data](https://registry.opendata.aws/depmap-omics-ccle/)). *Strengths:* unambiguously open, no patient/germline → bypasses the re-id gate; high-value for sibling reanalysis projects. *Weaknesses:* cell lines ≠ patient cohorts (scope note needed); not currently in plan.

**OmicsDI (EMBL-EBI/ELIXIR)** — cross-omics **dataset discovery index** spanning 20+ partner repositories, encoding every dataset in **schema.org** for Google Dataset Search; explicitly FAIR-motivated ([OmicsDI](https://www.omicsdi.org/); [EMBL-EBI](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/about/news/announcements/omicsdi-2017/)). *Strengths:* the closest *aggregator* analog; proven schema.org → Google Dataset Search pipeline. *Weaknesses:* breadth over depth — indexes open *and controlled* resources, no license-derivability verification, no re-id assessment, no rare-cancer curation. The catalog's "license-clear + datasheet-depth" angle is exactly what OmicsDI lacks.

**Google Dataset Search** — web-scale dataset discovery indexing any page with schema.org/Croissant metadata (700,000+ Croissant-tagged datasets) ([MLCommons Croissant](https://mlcommons.org/2024/03/croissant_metadata_announce/)). *Strengths:* free distribution channel — emit schema.org/Croissant and the catalog becomes findable globally. *Weaknesses:* no curation, license verification, or quality control — a discovery surface, not a competitor to curation.

**NCI CCDI Childhood Cancer Data Catalog** — federal inventory of childhood-cancer data resources, **222 datasets**, filterable by repository/registry/data type, with resource pages for Treehouse/Xena/cBioPortal/DepMap ([CCDI Catalog](https://datacatalog.ccdi.cancer.gov/)). **The most direct competitor on the catalog axis** (see 1.3). *Strengths:* authoritative, funded, broad pediatric coverage, API (CCDI Data Federation Resource). *Weaknesses:* **resource-level pointers, not per-dataset license-verified datasheets**; no `permitsDerivatives` clause citation, no re-id assessment, no Croissant/Datasheet artifacts, no rare-cancer *depth*. This is the gap to exploit *and* a ready adoption channel.

**Gabriella Miller Kids First DRC** — cloud ecosystem (Portal + CAVATICA) harmonizing germline+somatic pediatric cancer data, >34,000 participants ([Kids First DRC](https://kidsfirstdrc.org/about/); [CHOP D3b](https://d3b.center/kidsfirst/)). *Strengths:* major pediatric data hub. *Weaknesses:* predominantly **controlled-access** (germline WGS) — largely *out of scope* for this catalog and a good negative-control example for the access-tier gate.

**R2 Genomics (Amsterdam UMC)** — web platform for analysis/visualization of public expression datasets incl. pediatric/Ewing series. *Strengths:* popular for Ewing expression mining. *Weaknesses:* analysis platform, not a license/provenance catalog; reuse terms not machine-readable. (Plan does not mention it — worth a one-line awareness note.)

## 3. Gaps we can fill

1. **Per-dataset, machine-readable license + derivability verdict.** No competitor publishes a cited `permitsDerivatives` clause + license snapshot (hash + Wayback) per open Ewing dataset. cBioPortal/GDC/Xena leave this to the user. This is the catalog's core, defensible value.
2. **Rare-cancer depth where the big catalogs are broad.** CCDI/OmicsDI cover *everything* shallowly; a curated, exhaustively-vetted *Ewing* set (likely <15 truly-open datasets worldwide) is achievable, citable, and disproportionately useful to a small, underserved research community.
3. **Access-tier disambiguation as a first-class artifact.** The controlled-vs-open boundary (open expression but controlled raw tier; dbGaP-linked GEO series; ICGC DACO) is the #1 reuse hazard and is *nowhere* machine-encoded. A committed PASS/FLAG/EXCLUDE artifact per dataset is a genuinely new public good.
4. **Consent/ethics provenance for reuse.** Datasheets-for-Datasets composition/consent/collection sections, adapted for biomedical data, don't exist in any of these portals. This matters for responsible reuse of pediatric data.
5. **A FAIR bridge to general discovery.** Emitting schema.org (+ Bioschemas) + Croissant pushes vetted rare-cancer entries into Google Dataset Search/OmicsDI-style discovery — turning curation into distribution.
6. **A clean, typed feed for downstream reanalysis.** Sibling Elyos projects (KG, reanalysis, single-cell atlas) need a vetted, license-clear input list; no external catalog provides a derivability-guaranteed feed.
7. **Deduplication of re-posted cohorts.** The same Ewing cohort (e.g. Crompton/Broad) appears in GEO + cBioPortal + a publication; the `lineage{duplicateOf,supersedes}` model resolves double-counting that broad catalogs don't.

## 4. Differentiators to win

1. **"License-clear by construction."** Every entry carries a cited derivability clause + snapshot + access-tier evidence URL. This is the one-line pitch no competitor can claim, and it's verifiable.
2. **Conservatism as a feature, encoded as a blocking gate.** Controlled-access and NC databases are *categorically* excluded with CI fixtures proving it. For a privacy-sensitive pediatric cancer, "we will never document a controlled study as open" is a trust differentiator, not just a guardrail.
3. **Datasheet-depth, not pointer-depth.** Datasheets-for-Datasets + consent/ethics provenance + known-issues per dataset — a depth layer that sits *on top of* CCDI/OmicsDI rather than competing with their breadth (enabling contribution rather than rivalry).
4. **Rare-disease focus + replicable template.** Being the canonical open-data map for *one* rare cancer, built from a generalizable template, is more winnable than out-broadening NCI. The template itself is reusable across rare cancers (see §7).
5. **FAIR + dual-format machine readability.** schema.org/Bioschemas (biomedical discovery) *and* Croissant (ML/Google Dataset Search) from one canonical model — broader findability than any single-format competitor.
6. **Self-publishing independence.** Zenodo-DOI fallback means delivery never blocks on a third party — a structural advantage for a volunteer-driven good-deed project.

## 5. Claude API leverage

Current Anthropic model lineup for sizing the work: **Claude Opus 4.8** (`claude-opus-4-8`, $5/$25 per 1M in/out) for hard reasoning/judgment-adjacent drafting; **Claude Sonnet 4.6** (`claude-sonnet-4-6`, $3/$15) as the balanced default; **Claude Haiku 4.5** (`claude-haiku-4-5`, $1/$5) for cheap high-volume extraction. Two cost levers fit this batch-shaped, metadata-heavy workload especially well: **prompt caching** (cache reads ≈0.1× input price — cache the datasheet template, schema, and `policy-004` rules across every dataset) and the **Message Batches API** (50% off, ideal for non-interactive GEO/cBioPortal metadata harmonization runs).

**Where Claude clearly helps (human-reviewed):**
1. **Harmonize GEO/cBioPortal/ICGC metadata into the canonical record.** Messy ESummary/study payloads → structured `fields[]`, assay, platform, cohort size. Use **strict tool use / structured outputs** to force schema-valid JSON; Haiku for the bulk extraction, escalate to Sonnet on ambiguous records. Pairs with the M2 adapters.
2. **Draft Datasheets-for-Datasets prose.** Generate first-draft motivation/composition/collection/uses sections from the canonical record + the source publication, for human editing. High leverage, low risk (descriptive text, human-reviewed, never a verdict).
3. **License-clause *extraction and surfacing* (not determination).** Pull the candidate redistribution/derivatives clause + quote it with a citation so the human reviewer adjudicates faster. Claude surfaces evidence; the human decides `permitsDerivatives`.
4. **Dataset deduplication / lineage mapping.** Flag likely duplicate cohorts across GEO/cBioPortal/publications (`lineage.duplicateOf`) by reasoning over accessions, sample counts, and author overlap — proposals only, human-confirmed.
5. **Molecular/fusion annotation drafting from the publication** (EWSR1-FLI1 vs variant fusions, assay, cohort N) — strictly *sourced from* the paper with citation, never inferred; flagged for technical-reviewer verification.
6. **Croissant/schema.org JSON-LD generation + self-validation**, then checked by the pinned validator in CI.
7. **Coverage triage assistant for `coverage-005`** — sweep a source for candidate Ewing series and pre-classify likely access tier, producing a worklist (every item still requires the human gate).

**Where Claude must NOT decide (hard boundary — reinforce in the plan):**
- **Access-tier and license/controlled-access determination stay human-verified.** Claude may *extract and quote* the access designation and license clause; the binding PASS/FLAG/EXCLUDE on access tier, `permitsDerivatives`, and re-identification is the named gate reviewer's, recorded with cited evidence. A model hallucinating "open" or "derivatives allowed" is exactly the High-impact risk the plan rates — never let the gate verdict be model-authored.
- **Never ingest controlled-access or patient-level data into a prompt.** Only public, aggregate *metadata* may reach the API. This must be an explicit rule in the access protocol: the inspection step feeds Claude headers/summaries only, never expression matrices, VCFs, or individual-level rows — consistent with the Elyos "never write patient data" and "metadata-only" guardrails.
- **No clinical interpretation.** Claude drafts descriptive metadata only; any drift toward prognosis/treatment framing triggers the `high`-tier escalation, not silent generation.
- **Re-identification reasoning is advisory, not authoritative** — Claude can flag small-cell/germline signals; it cannot clear a dataset.

## 6. Ten concrete optimizations

1. **Drop TARGET to an expected-negative and re-scope `gdc-017` to ICGC BOCA / GDC CGCI.** Stop spending adapter effort on a source with no Ewing cohort (§1.1).
2. **Add St. Jude PeCan and DepMap/CCLE as named candidate families.** Cell-line data (DepMap) gives the *fastest* gate-PASS pilot (no patient/germline) and de-risks M0 (§1.2).
3. **Emit schema.org/Dataset (+ Bioschemas) as the primary record, Croissant as a secondary projection.** Widen "Findable" into the biomedical *and* Google Dataset Search ecosystems for near-zero extra cost (§1.6).
4. **Make CCDI contribution a first-class adoption channel.** Submitting vetted entries to NCI's CCDI catalog is a concrete, reachable third-party adoption path that fixes the fragile `verifiedNeed: false` metric (§1.3, §1.8).
5. **Define deterministic per-source version keys now** (GEO accession+update-date; cBioPortal study+data-version; ICGC release#), so `refresh-020` drift detection has something to diff (§1.7).
6. **Cache the template + schema + `policy-004` across all datasets via prompt caching; run metadata harmonization through the Batches API.** ~0.1× cached-input cost and 50% batch discount on the highest-volume step.
7. **Tier the model: Haiku for bulk metadata extraction, Sonnet default, Opus for ambiguous license/lineage reasoning** — with strict structured outputs to keep records schema-valid.
8. **Cite a basis for the k<5 small-cell threshold and make it policy-tunable per source;** reframe the re-id gate as a *source-appropriateness* check, not a transformation (§1.5).
9. **Reframe GEO's default in `policy-004`** to "attribute submitter, treat summary text as the submitter's, record provenance" rather than "public domain" (§1.4).
10. **Build a one-page comparison matrix (this catalog vs CCDI/OmicsDI/cBioPortal/Xena)** as a published artifact — doubles as positioning and as evidence for partner outreach.

## 7. Parallel & perpendicular spin-offs

- **`ewsr1-fli1-knowledge-graph` (primary downstream).** The catalog's typed, license-clear feed is the KG's vetted input list; ship the `kg-handoff-025` export contract early so the KG is the *first confirmed consumer* (an internal adoption that satisfies a success metric without external partners).
- **`ewing-expression-reanalysis`.** Consumes the catalog's open, derivable expression datasets (GEO/Treehouse/DepMap) with provenance attached — the catalog answers "what may we legally reanalyze?" before reanalysis starts.
- **`cancer-dataset-datasheets` / `open-data-datasheets` (pattern sibling).** Generalize the datasheet template + three-part gate into a reusable library; this project is the oncology specialization, that one the substrate.
- **`open-cohort-catalog` (generalized rare-cancer template).** The biggest multiplier: the same gate + canonical model + adapters retargeted to *any* rare cancer (rhabdomyosarcoma, osteosarcoma, DSRCT, neuroblastoma). Ewing proves the template; the template scales the good deed.
- **MCP server for researchers.** Expose the vetted catalog as an **MCP server** so any agent (Claude included) can query "open, derivable Ewing expression datasets with EWSR1-FLI1, attribution recorded" and get license-clear results with provenance — turning the static index into a live, agent-queryable tool. Natural fit with the Elyos agent-neutral core.
- **Croissant/schema.org "publish-back" bot.** A small tool that contributes catalog entries to Google Dataset Search, OmicsDI-style aggregators, and CCDI — converting curation into distribution automatically.
- **License-snapshot-as-a-service.** The committed license-page-snapshot + SHA-256 + Wayback mechanism (`snapshot-010`) is independently useful to *any* open-data project; could spin out as a tiny reusable utility.
- **"Controlled-vs-open" reuse-hazard checklist.** Publish the access-tier decision methodology (dbGaP/EGA/DACO/linked-raw-tier traps) as a standalone community resource — high citation potential, low effort, seeds outreach.

## 8. Open questions for the maintainer

1. **Adoption channel:** Will you pursue **CCDI contribution** and/or **sibling-KG consumption** as the *primary* delivered-outcome path, rather than relying on the Zenodo self-publish fallback (which is not third-party adoption)?
2. **TARGET:** Given TARGET demonstrably excludes Ewing, do you drop it from the source families and the `gdc-017` adapter at M0, retargeting GDC effort to ICGC BOCA / CGCI?
3. **Cell lines in scope?** DepMap/CCLE Ewing data is unambiguously open and bypasses the re-id gate — is *cell-line* (non-patient) data in scope, and if so does it become the pilot to de-risk M0?
4. **Metadata standard:** Adopt schema.org/Bioschemas as the primary machine record (Croissant secondary), or stay Croissant-first? This decides your discovery ecosystem.
5. **Re-id gate scope:** Do you accept reframing the re-identification check as a *source-appropriateness* gate (the catalog never transforms data), and cite/parameterize the k threshold?
6. **Linked controlled raw tiers** (St. Jude PeCan-open + controlled-raw; GEO-open + dbGaP-raw): confirm the default — "document the open part, explicitly note 'controlled raw tier excluded'" — and who signs it off.
7. **GEO licensing default:** Endorse the "attribute submitter, don't treat as blanket public domain" rule in `policy-004`?
8. **Reviewer recruitment:** The combined license + genomic-governance reviewer is the project's hard blocker — is there a realistic named candidate, given nothing proceeds past the gate without one?
9. **MCP server:** Is an agent-queryable MCP interface on the roadmap, or strictly out of scope for v1?
